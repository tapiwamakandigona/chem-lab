## Model targets derived from the sources

- Keep the 50 mL burette visibly long and slender relative to the 250 mL flask:
  ~3.5–4.1× the flask height from tube top to jet.
- Retort rod and burette diameters should be comparable at a glance (roughly
  12 and ≤14 mm in the real apparatus), with a broad heavy base rather than a
  thin decorative plate.
- The flask should read as 85 mm wide × 145 mm high: conical shoulder, narrow
  ~34 mm internal neck, strengthened rim and physically plausible glass wall.
- Clamp contact points need protective jaws/sleeves and must visibly hold the
  burette vertical without intersecting its scale or crushing the tube.
- Place a white tile under the flask; align the flask mouth under the jet.
- Setup validity requires: stand/base placed, clamp on rod, burette vertical
  in clamp, white tile below, prepared flask centred under jet, tap closed.
- Animation priorities: weighty placement, short magnetic snap, glass-safe
  settle (no bounce through solids), clamp jaws closing, liquid slosh tied to
  flask motion, and reduced-motion fallback.

## Review protocol

For each changed model capture: front, side, three-quarter left/right, 390×844
portrait and 844×390 landscape. Check silhouettes, intersections, scale,
support/centre of mass, contact shadows, glass/liquid boundary and control
reachability. A single hero angle is not completion evidence.

## Hands-on rollout map

Ranked by educational value and safety/validity decisions, not by easiest
animation:

1. **Titration (implemented first):** stand → clamp → burette → white tile →
   prepared flask; then filling/funnel/tip-air-gap technique.
2. **Simple distillation:** clamp/support round-bottom flask and condenser,
   choose upward cooling-water direction, place receiver, add anti-bumping
   granules before heat. Highest remaining setup-validity value.
3. **Gas collection / peroxide kinetics:** stopper + delivery tube + gas
   syringe alignment and leak-free connections before mixing.
4. **Electrochemical cells:** electrodes, salt bridge and voltmeter leads with
   polarity; direct connection choices affect the reading.
5. **Gravimetric heating:** tripod/gauze/crucible/lid arrangement, balance
   transfer and cool-before-weigh sequence.
6. **Solubility / enthalpy:** thermometer depth without touching glass,
   calorimeter lid/cup placement and measured solid transfer.
7. **Chromatography:** baseline, sample spotting, solvent below baseline and
   vertical paper placement.
8. **Flame / qualitative / organic:** rack, test tube, dropper/reagent choices
   and contamination-safe tool placement.
9. **Iodine clock / iodine-rate:** measured reagent staging, timed transfer,
   quench and subsequent titration bench setup.

Every rollout should reuse the same ordered dependency model, magnetic snap,
pointer-capture discipline, keyboard/touch alternative, validity interlock and
reduced-motion behaviour. Practical-specific chemistry owns the order and
consequences.
